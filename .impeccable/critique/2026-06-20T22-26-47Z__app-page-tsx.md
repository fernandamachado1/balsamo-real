#### Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment**: The page does not feel broken, but it still reads as AI-assisted landing page design in a few ways. The sections are too evenly spaced, too symmetrical, and too similar in composition. Hero, product, benefits, history, and revenda all use the same editorial split-section DNA, so the scroll starts to feel procedural instead of curated. The visual language is on-brand, but the composition is over-programmed. The biggest slop signal is not the palette, it is the repetition of the same structural formula with only background color swaps.

#### Overall Impression

The home has a strong craft mood and a credible brand presence, but the structure is too repetitive for the amount of content it carries. It feels like a series of premium sections rather than a single page that builds a clear decision from top to bottom. The biggest opportunity is to reduce section sameness and make the page decide harder: what should a visitor understand first, what should they do next, and which path is primary, buyer or reseller.

#### What's Working

- The brand atmosphere is coherent. The palette, photography, and typography all support the leathercraft context without looking generic.
- The hero establishes tone quickly. It communicates premium, artisanal, and direct-sales intent in the first screen.
- The alternating light and dark section blocks give the page a sense of chaptering, which is useful for a story-driven landing page.

#### Priority Issues

- **[P1] Repeated section grammar**: Hero, product, benefits, history, and revenda are all built from the same split-section pattern with a label, headline, body copy, and image. This makes the scroll feel machine-assembled rather than intentionally paced. **Why it matters**: users stop feeling progression and start scanning for the next similar block. **Fix**: collapse or merge at least one section, and give each remaining section a distinct role, such as hero, proof, product detail, and conversion. **Suggested command**: `polish homepage` or `layout homepage`.
- **[P1] Revenda dominates too early**: The hero leads with `Quero revender`, while the product and buyer path are secondary. **Why it matters**: a first-time visitor may not know whether this is a product page, a reseller page, or both. **Fix**: make the top CTA neutral and product-first, then keep revenda as the secondary business path. **Suggested command**: `clarify homepage`.
- **[P2] Too much vertical commitment before conversion**: The page uses full-height and long scroll sections before the user reaches a concrete next step. **Why it matters**: the user has to invest a lot of attention before understanding the offer. **Fix**: add a compact product summary or catalog entry point higher up, and shorten one of the mid-page narrative sections. **Suggested command**: `distill homepage`.
- **[P2] Copy is specific but not decision-oriented enough**: The copy explains the brand story and benefits, but does not make it easy to compare kits or understand who each kit is for from the home. **Why it matters**: first-time buyers need a faster decision scaffold. **Fix**: add a short kit-selection cue or use-case summary near the product section. **Suggested command**: `clarify homepage`.
- **[P3] Decorative texture load is high**: Background overlays, gradients, and textured treatments appear across multiple sections. **Why it matters**: the page looks polished, but the visual surface gets busy and starts to flatten hierarchy. **Fix**: keep the texture in fewer hero moments and let the rest breathe. **Suggested command**: `quieter homepage`.

#### Persona Red Flags

- **First-time buyer**: The hero leads with revenda, the page does not quickly explain which kit to buy first, and the home does not surface a concise buying decision. A new customer has to read several sections before reaching the catalog path.
- **Reseller / atelier owner**: The page supports this persona well conceptually, but the repeated section structure buries the strongest reseller signal under a lot of narrative. The revenda section should be more direct and earlier.
- **Mobile scroller**: Tall section blocks and repeated split layouts create fatigue on a phone. The page asks for a lot of vertical commitment before the user can compare or act.

#### Minor Observations

- The hero button pair is visually balanced, but the business priority is not. One CTA should likely win.
- The history section is emotionally credible, but it is long relative to the amount of actionable information it adds.
- The product and benefits sections overlap enough that they can probably be consolidated.
- The page would benefit from one denser, content-rich section instead of five evenly weighted ones.

#### Questions to Consider

- What should be the primary reading order here, brand story first, product first, or reseller first?
- Which section would you be most comfortable collapsing or merging so the page feels less repetitive?
- Should the top CTA be buyer-first, reseller-first, or split by intent?
